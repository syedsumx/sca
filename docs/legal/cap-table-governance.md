## 3. Proposed standard terms (not executed)

These are proposals for the board to adopt or change. They describe a standard structure. They do not describe any grant that exists.

### 3.1 Option pool

- **Proposal:** reserve 10% to 15% of capitalization as an unallocated option pool. The board sets the exact percentage and the basis for it (for example, fully diluted).
- The pool is reserved under the equity plan. Shares in the pool are not granted until the board approves each grant.
- `[TO CONFIRM: the percentage the board sets, and the basis of the percentage]`

### 3.2 Vesting for employee grants

- **Proposal (standard, not executed):** four years, with a one-year cliff. Nothing vests before the first anniversary. After the cliff, the remainder vests monthly, or on the schedule the plan and the grant agreement specify.
- A grant's vesting schedule is fixed in the grant agreement. A change to it needs a board resolution and a written amendment.

### 3.3 Restricted stock and the Section 83(b) election

This section describes the filing protocol for a U.S. taxpayer who receives restricted stock. Whether the rules below apply depends on the recipient's tax residence and the company's jurisdiction. `[TO CONFIRM: counsel and a tax adviser to confirm applicability in each recipient's jurisdiction]`

**Why it matters.** Restricted stock subject to vesting is generally taxed when the restriction lifts, based on the value then. A Section 83(b) election, filed in time, lets the recipient instead be taxed at the grant, on the value then. The election is a deadline-driven filing. The deadline is strict. `[TO CONFIRM: counsel to confirm whether any relief is available after a missed deadline]`

**Deadline.** The election must be filed with the IRS within **30 days after the date of the transfer**. For restricted stock, the transfer is the grant. `[TO CONFIRM: counsel to confirm the computation of the 30-day period, including whether a weekend or holiday extends it, against current IRS instructions]`

**Filing protocol (for each recipient, each grant).**

1. **Decide before the grant.** Tell the recipient in writing, before the grant date, whether an election is available and that the deadline is 30 days from the grant. The recipient decides whether to file. The company does not file on the recipient's behalf unless the recipient has signed a written authority `[TO CONFIRM: counsel's view on that authority]`.
2. **Prepare the election.** The written statement must contain the information the Treasury regulations require, which `[TO CONFIRM: counsel to list from the current regulations and form instructions]` includes:
   - the recipient's name, address and taxpayer identification number;
   - a description of the property (number of shares, class, issuer);
   - the date of the transfer and the taxable year for which the election is made;
   - the nature of the restriction, including the vesting schedule and forfeiture terms;
   - the fair market value of the property at transfer, and the amount paid for it;
   - a statement that copies were furnished to the company and to any other required person.
3. **Support the fair market value.** The value must be supported. For a company that is not publicly traded, the valuation basis is `[TO CONFIRM: the valuation method and the date of the supporting valuation]`.
4. **File with the IRS** at the office that the current instructions specify for the recipient's location, by the delivery method the instructions require `[TO CONFIRM: filing office and delivery method per current IRS instructions]`. Keep proof of filing.
5. **Give a copy to the company.** The company keeps a copy in the grant file. The recipient keeps a copy with the tax records for that year, and attaches a copy to the return as the instructions require `[TO CONFIRM: attachment requirement]`.
6. **Record it.** The cap table register records the grant date, the election's deadline, the date of filing and the proof of filing. The register is the company's evidence that the protocol was followed.

## 4. Governance controls (proposal)

1. **Board approval.** Every issuance, grant, repurchase and vesting change is approved by a board resolution that names the recipient, the number of shares, the class, the price, the vesting schedule and the grant date. No grant takes effect without a resolution.
2. **Written agreements.** Each grant has a signed grant agreement and, for restricted stock, a stock purchase agreement, both in the grant file before the grant date.
3. **Register.** One person keeps the cap table register. Changes to it are made only on the strength of a resolution and an agreement. A second person checks each change `[TO CONFIRM: who checks]`.
4. **Reconciliation.** The register is reconciled to the charter, the board minutes and the signed agreements at least quarterly `[TO CONFIRM: frequency and owner]`.
5. **Securities law.** Each issuance and grant is checked against an exemption from registration in each relevant jurisdiction. For U.S. compensatory grants, the usual exemption and its conditions are for counsel to confirm `[TO CONFIRM: securities counsel's review of each issuance and grant, and of any filing required]`.
6. **Transfer terms.** Confirm which transfer restrictions apply to common stock and awards, such as a right of first refusal, co-sale rights or a repurchase right on termination `[TO CONFIRM: terms in the charter, bylaws and agreements]`.
7. **Minutes and consents.** Board and stockholder actions are written up and signed. `[TO CONFIRM: where the minute book is kept]`
8. **Changes to this framework.** Any change to the standard terms in section 3 is a board resolution, recorded with the date.
